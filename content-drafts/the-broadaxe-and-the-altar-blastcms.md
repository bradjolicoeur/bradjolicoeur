## The Book

Born in Maine and raised in the hard, devout parishes of Quebec, Alex Jolicoeur is the eldest of ten — a firstborn son groomed for the priesthood, built instead for a broadaxe. He has a carpenter's strength, a protector's temper, and a stubborn conscience that will not bend to father, Church, or law. When his fury at the mistreatment of the weak leaves him no safe future at home, his own father orders him to run.

He flees to Maine's North Woods with nothing but his hands.

What follows is the long, hard-forged life of a runaway: blacksmithing in Maine's logging camps, breaking a runaway twelve-horse team on the wheat plains of the Dakotas, riveting steel through the lean years of the Depression, and topping the last old-growth giants of the Pacific Northwest. When a long-delayed draft notice finally catches up to him, it carries him across the ocean to wartime England — and into the path of Peggy, the woman who will teach him that the hardest thing he will ever build is a home he doesn't have to run from.

Sweeping from the Chaudière River valley to the mill towns of New England, *The Broadaxe and the Altar* is a historical novel of exile and endurance, faith and defiance, and the lifelong reckoning between a man's duty to everyone he loves and his need to remain, above all, his own.

## For Readers Of

Character-driven historical fiction, family sagas, and stories of work, faith, and belonging across the twentieth century.

## About the Author

Bradley Jolicoeur is the grandson of Alex and Peggy Jolicoeur, whose lives inspired this novel. By trade, he is a software architect.
